## Primary audience

The primary audience is senior technical hiring decision-makers evaluating me for hands-on product-engineering, Staff, Principal or technical-leadership roles.

This includes:

- engineering managers;
- heads or VPs of engineering;
- CTOs and technical founders;
- Staff and Principal engineers involved in interviews;
- senior product engineers.

They are trying to assess judgement, autonomy, breadth, communication, product sense and technical depth.

## Secondary audience

### Recruiters and talent partners

They need a quick, credible explanation of what I do and why my experience is relevant. They should not need to understand Morris's symbolic architecture to find the useful evidence.

### Curious engineers

They may arrive through GitHub, a shared project or search. They are likely to explore architecture, failures, code and unusual questions in more depth.

### Former colleagues and collaborators

They may use the site as a concise reference for my work and approach.

## Audience needs

Relevant visitors need to answer some combination of:

- What kind of engineer is he?
- Can he still build, or has he moved entirely into management?
- Can he handle ambiguity without heavy process?
- Does he understand customers and products as well as systems?
- How quickly can he learn outside his established stack?
- Does he create leverage for other engineers?
- Can he explain complex work clearly?
- Is the breadth real or just a list of keywords?

## Audience constraints

- Many readers will spend less than two minutes initially.
- Some will be on a phone.
- Some will not be deeply technical.
- Technical interviewers may return later for a twenty-minute deep dive.
- Readers should not be required to create an account or request access merely to understand a project.
- Private repository access is supplementary evidence, not the main experience.

## Audience priority

When needs conflict, prioritise:

1. relevant senior engineering decision-makers;
2. technical interviewers and peers;
3. recruiters and general hiring readers;
4. broader public visitors.

This priority does not justify inaccessible language. It guides how much technical depth the product should support.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A hiring manager can understand the core proposition quickly.
- [ ] A senior engineer can reach meaningful technical depth.
- [ ] Recruiters can identify relevant experience without reading every experiment.
- [ ] Public visitors can browse without private access or specialist tooling.
