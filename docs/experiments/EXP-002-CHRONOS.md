## Central question

What if history could be explored as a connected temporal system rather than as isolated articles and timelines?

## Why this matters

The project began with a practical request from my wife: she wanted a more joined-up way to understand what was happening across places, people and events at the same time. Existing historical resources are often rich but fragmented.

## Narrative spine

1. The original request and why ordinary timelines were insufficient.
2. The hypothesis that temporal relationships should be first-class data.
3. Early modelling experiments around events, entities, dates and uncertainty.
4. Evolution toward a temporal knowledge graph and mobile experience.
5. Difficulties around incomplete dates, conflicting sources and scale.
6. What the project taught me about data modelling, product scope and historical ambiguity.

## Technical areas to explain

- temporal graph modelling;
- uncertain and approximate dates;
- entity resolution;
- source provenance;
- relationship traversal;
- indexing and query patterns;
- mobile presentation of dense connected data;
- boundaries between authored knowledge and generated assistance.
