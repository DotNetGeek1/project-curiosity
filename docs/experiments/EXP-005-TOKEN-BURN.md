## Central question

Can a small set of mechanics produce a mobile rogue-like game that is simple, fair and extremely difficult to put down?

## Why this matters

Token Burn gives the notebook a different kind of engineering story. It explores feedback loops, balancing, progression, mobile constraints and product feel rather than infrastructure or AI.

## Narrative spine

1. The desire to build something compact and genuinely fun.
2. The initial mechanic and why it seemed promising.
3. Rapid prototypes and play-test feedback.
4. Balancing difficulty, progression and replayability.
5. Mobile interaction, state and performance decisions.
6. What the project taught me about iteration, restraint and player behaviour.

## Technical areas to explain

- game-state modelling;
- deterministic versus random behaviour;
- progression and balancing systems;
- save-state and offline behaviour;
- animation and feedback;
- mobile performance;
- testing game rules;
- analytics boundaries if any play data is collected.

## Evidence to collect

- gameplay capture;
- mechanic diagrams;
- progression or balancing notes;
- before-and-after examples from play testing;
- code excerpts for a rule or state system;
- examples of mechanics removed after testing;
- performance observations on representative devices.

## Claims requiring verification

- addictiveness or retention;
- player numbers;
- measured engagement;
- platform availability;
- originality of individual mechanics.

## Confidentiality risks

Low. Confirm ownership and licensing of art, sound, fonts and third-party game assets.

## Open questions

- What is the core loop in one sentence?
- Which design change had the largest effect on play?
- Is there enough measured play-test evidence for a full experiment story?
- What does this project show about my engineering approach that the AI projects do not?

## Publication target

Short launch entry, expanding to a full experiment if the gameplay and iteration evidence is strong enough.
