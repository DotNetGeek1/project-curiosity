> Good design should support understanding, not compete with it.

The site should be visually distinctive, but its credibility comes from reliable engineering and clear communication.

A beautiful interaction that is slow, fragile, inaccessible or difficult to maintain is not a successful design.

A plain interaction that makes a complex system understandable may be.

This principle does not mean aesthetics are unimportant. The visual language should communicate care, curiosity and technical seriousness. It means appearance must remain accountable to the product.

## Test

A visual or interactive decision should survive these questions:

- Does it improve comprehension?
- Does it preserve accessibility?
- Does it remain usable on modest hardware?
- Does it fail safely?
- Is the maintenance cost proportionate?
- Would the page still work without it?
