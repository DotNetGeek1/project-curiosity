## Purpose

The About page explains how I became the kind of engineer represented by the experiments. It should connect my career, working style and interests without reproducing my CV.

## Primary Outcome

The reader understands the continuity between my professional experience, technical leadership and curiosity-led side projects.

## Page Structure

### 1. Opening

A concise first-person statement about solving difficult problems, learning unfamiliar technologies and remaining hands-on throughout technical leadership roles.

### 2. How I Work

Describe the habits that recur across my work:

- starting with the problem rather than the framework;
- using prototypes to reduce uncertainty;
- building reusable foundations;
- collaborating across engineering, product and business roles;
- staying close to implementation;
- improving systems and developer experience without waiting for a formal initiative.

### 3. How I Ended Up Here

Use a thematic narrative rather than a complete timeline. Useful themes include:

- early systems and support experience;
- application and product engineering;
- architecture and technical leadership;
- cloud and platform engineering;
- applied AI;
- personal experiments.

Employment dates and detailed role history belong in the CV.

### 4. What I Am Looking For

A short, direct explanation of the work environment and problems I am interested in. Avoid a generic list of benefits or employer requirements.

### 5. Outside Work

Explain the role of physics, experimentation, games and speculative engineering in how I learn. This section should add personality while remaining relevant to the engineering story.

### 6. Practical Links

Provide routes to:

- CV;
- experiments;
- GitHub;
- LinkedIn where retained;
- contact.

## Content Rules

- First-person voice throughout.
- Do not describe me in third person.
- Do not use a copied CV profile as the entire page.
- Avoid a long skills inventory.
- Avoid autobiography that does not help the reader understand how I work.
- Every broad claim should connect to an experiment, role or concrete example.

## Visual Guidance

A portrait is optional. If used, it should feel natural and secondary to the written story.

The page may include a compact annotated timeline, but it must not become an interactive résumé or require horizontal scrolling.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] The page explains my working style in first person.
- [ ] It connects professional experience and side projects.
- [ ] It does not duplicate the full CV.
- [ ] It includes a clear route to experiments and contact.
- [ ] It avoids generic personal-brand language.
- [ ] It remains useful without a portrait or decorative timeline.
